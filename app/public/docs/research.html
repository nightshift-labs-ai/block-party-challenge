Event facts that matter for the choice (event page):

  • One winner per creator (5 Creator Partnership Awards). Gold $800, Silver $500 and Bronze $300 go only to those 5 winners. So the field to beat is the teams that pick the same creator, not all ~70 teams.
  • Judging: 30% business model, 30% audience fit, 20% originality, 20% trends. Up to +10 for DevOps (CI/CD, health checks, load test, rollback, autoscaling on Render).
  • GAMST is a "special creator" ("he won't advertise a product he doesn't like"). [UNVERIFIED] whether he has his own award slot.
  • One of the judges owns "Dose Online", which the page calls Korea's #1 game. Expect Korean game-industry judging taste.

1. LOVE SOO: Korean-American lifestyle/food (US audience)

  • YT @lovesoostrong 593K subs · TikTok 390.5K followers, 19.5M likes · IG @lovesoooffical 152K.
  • Niche: "50-year-old Korean woman in the US, married to a younger man". Was a vegan Korean cooking/mukbang channel. Since 2025 it is the "14 years vegan, first time eating X" series, plus gym/powerlifting ("50 & just getting started", coach, @power4strong) and couple storytime.
  • Language: English with Korean words. Audience is likely US, women 25 to 55 [UNVERIFIED, no demographics public].
  • Top videos: "14 yrs VEGAN, first time eating STEAK" 6.1M · "Show me the life hack" 2.9M · vegan bibim naengmyeon 5.3M (old long-form). Recent shorts get 20K to 150K. Recent long-form gets 5K to 100K (falling).
  • Existing business: brand deals (#PopeyesPartner, AG1). Agency email @undercurrent.net. Linktree has no merch or app.
  • What fans would pay for: Korean recipe/meal-plan subscription, "strong at 50" women's strength program, curated K-food/K-beauty shop. These are adult US payers with the highest willingness to pay of the five. But it is a poor fit for games.

2. Tushar Shetty: Indian dance choreographer

  • YT 671K subs · IG 710K followers (third-party trackers say 711K to 731K) · Bollywood TV dance shows (Super Dancer 4, India's Best Dancer).
  • Content: Hindi/English shorts dancing to Bollywood songs with celebrities (Nora Fatehi 28M, Pushpa "cap trick" 15M, an Instagram ad 27M), plus behind-the-scenes vlogs with child dancer Florina Gogoi (#FLOTUS 9.6M). Recent shorts get 20K to 700K. Vlogs get 25K to 150K.
  • Audience: India, teens to young adults, aspiring dancers [UNVERIFIED]. Earns from workshops (Pune, Bangkok) and brand collabs.
  • Fans would pay for online choreography classes/workshop tickets, cheap in INR.
  • His suggested product (a dance game with fan video ranking) needs webcam/pose ML or video upload and moderation. The music is copyrighted Bollywood tracks, which the rules ban. Poor fit for us.

  • Audience: Uzbek-speaking small sellers and women who want to make AI content/ads [UNVERIFIED].
  • Fans pay for the AI course (already sold) and maybe an Uzbek AI-ad/caption tool. That tool needs paid LLM/image APIs, which we can't use. Low purchasing power. Couldn't check post-level engagement (Instagram login wall).

4. Kkwallu (꽐루): Korean Brawl Stars/Roblox gamer, kids

  • YT @min_1923 "틱톡★꽐루" 273K subs (Namu wiki says 280K) · TikTok 164.3K followers, 5.6M likes.
  • Content: Brawl Stars shorts (median 241K views over the last 400; top 2.7M) and a long-form "[Minigame #1 to #43]" series of custom Brawl Stars minigames with viewers (median 46K, top 413K). Last long video was 2025-03-29 and the last short 2026-08-13. The YouTube channel looks inactive for about 3 months. The TikTok bio says "now doing Roblox".
  • Language: Korean (bio also offers Chinese for ads). Audience: Korean elementary/middle schoolers [UNVERIFIED, inferred from content].

6. GAMST (special): 감스트, 3.06M subs

  • Korean streamer/YouTuber (also SOOP). Football (Man Utd fan, Korea national team), FC Online pack openings, LoL, mukbang and comedy. Videos get 0.4M to 4.6M. Audience: Korean men aged 15 to 35 [UNVERIFIED].
  • Fit: a free football/FC prediction league or "Gamst psychic" pick'em with fan rankings ("Worlds Finals prediction success" 2.7M). It has to stay free with no betting, to avoid gambling. A demanding creator, and the prize slot is unclear. Not first choice.

Recommendation: GodDot (Dname)

Why: (1) Highest views per video of the five creators (median 1.7M). (2) His content already is game design: we turn his video formats into playable web games, which is our strength, needs no webcam, ML or paid APIs, and is original IP with no Brawl Stars or Mojang branding. (3) "I leave it up to you" plus a non-obvious channel probably means fewer competing teams than Kkwallu, whose suggested "mini-games hub" will attract every game team [UNVERIFIED guess]. (4) "Subscribe and join the game" means fans already want to play with him. We give them a place to do it between videos. Risks: rare uploads, a minor audience (payments → parents/vouchers/ads), and no public demographics. Prize events must stay free-entry and sponsor-funded so they don't count as gambling or lotteries (Korean law). Korea's rating rule for online games (GRAC) needs a check before commercial launch.

Concept A: "디네임 챌린지 아레나" (Dname Challenge Arena), top pick

  • Core loop: a weekly "episode" of 3 to 5 quick browser party games straight from his formats: Find the Diamond (grid-dig luck), Greed Game (take less than your neighbor), Block Telepathy (match the crowd's pick), Fake Lava (spot the fake), and a Don't-Say-ㄹ word game. Real-time rooms of 10 to 100 players (WebSocket) or async vs. ghost scores. A season leaderboard and a weekly "final" streamed and recorded by Dname, whose clips become his next short (content flywheel: game → video → game).
  • Money: free to play. Cosmetic blocky skins/emotes (₩1,000 to 5,000), a ₩4,900/month "fan pass" (extra lives, private rooms for friend groups, badge), sponsor-branded episodes (food/snack brands already in his niche: chicken, lava, emerald). Prizes come from the sponsor, never from players. Kid-safe ads on non-pass plays.
  • Why these fans: the same games they watch at 2M views, now playable with Dname, with a real chance to appear in his video.

Concept B: "눈치게임 TV" (crowd-psychology party game for classrooms and friend groups)

  • Core loop: Jackbox-style. One screen hosts and phones join with a 4-letter code. Rounds of balance games, telepathy, the trust/greed dilemma and roulette. Dname voice lines and stickers.
  • Money: free basic pack. ₩3,900 to 9,900 one-time theme packs; creator-made weekly question packs on subscription; school/academy group license. Jackbox proves people pay for this.
  • Why: his top formats (balance game, telepathy, trust) are social games kids play in class and on Discord.

Concept C: "Dname Lucky Mine" (daily one-minute puzzle, Wordle-style)

  • One shared daily dig/minesweeper-like puzzle ("find the diamond in 1 minute") with a shareable emoji result, streaks and a friend league. Cheap to build, and the Wordle share loop drives growth.
  • Money: ads plus ₩2,900/month for an ad-free archive and hint tokens. Sponsored "golden block" days. This is weaker on the business model, so use it as a retention feature inside A.

Runner-up: Kkwallu: original "꽐루 미니게임 랜드"

A hub of 10 to 15 original top-down arena minigames (avoid the meteors, tag with 9 chasers, build a path and fight, protect the cube), adapted from his #1 to #43 series with original characters and no Supercell IP. Wed/Sat "viewer vs. Kkwallu" event rooms, cosmetics and a season pass. Good fit, but YouTube is inactive (moving to Roblox), there is IP risk, and probably the most competition. (Dark horse on business model: LOVE SOO's "Strong at 50" Korean meal and strength subscription for US women. Adult payers, but not a game, and it plays away from our strength.)

Trends: why now (sources checked 2026-11-06)

  • Korean kids live on short-form. Korea Press Foundation's "2025 Tweens' and Teens' Media Use" (2,674 students from grade 4 to grade 12, fieldwork by Korea Gallup, released 2026-01-07) found 200.6 min of online video a day (elementary 143.6 min, middle school 233.7 min). 49.1% watch short-form every day (0.2% in the 2022 survey). Most-used platforms: Instagram Reels 37.2%, YouTube 35.8%, YouTube Shorts 16.5%. Source: Korea Times, 2026-01-07, https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/society/20260107/korean-teens-watch-online-videos-3-hours-daily-half-view-short-form-content-every-day
  • YouTube Shorts dominates short-form across ages: 93.4% of Korean short-form users use it (KOCCA 2025 Content Usage Survey, 6,554 people aged 10+). Source: https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025121510582925254 (Asia Economy, Dec 2025; summary, not the primary report).
  • Platforms are pushing "play with your audience". In Sep 2025 YouTube added Playables minigames to Live: 75+ casual games that the creator plays while chatting. It also tested a live leaderboard of the top 50 participating viewers. Source: TechCrunch 2025-09-16, https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/youtube-live-gets-a-major-update-introduces-dual-format-streaming-minigames-and-more/ → Our angle is the step after that. With Playables only the creator plays. In Block Party, every viewer plays on their own phone, in the creator's own formats.
  • Daily shared puzzles with a share grid (the Wordle pattern) are now a standard retention loop. Our Daily Challenge uses it to turn one-off Shorts views into a daily habit. (No figures claimed.)
