Block Party Challenge: business model (updated 2026-11-07)

Partner creator: GodDot / Dname (YouTube @d_name0). Every number below that is not a public count is an assumption, marked (A). The model is in tools/model.py, so judges and the creator can change the inputs and re-run it.

1. Who pays, and why they would

Payer What they get Why they pay
Brands (snacks, kids' food, stationery, mobile games) A sponsored weekly "Dname Final" episode: branded round, logo on the big screen, the prize. Built in the prototype: the banner on every screen, round 1 asks the sponsor's question (e.g. "Best snack flavour?"), and a downloadable sponsor report after the episode (players, minutes of play, answers, vote split; no personal data) They already buy kids' attention through creators. Here it comes as play time, not a 5-second mention.
Fans' parents (Fan Pass ₩4,900/mo) Private rooms for friend groups, custom question packs, cosmetics, a season badge. Demo built: /?pass=demo lets the host type their own questions (no payment in the prototype) The kid wants to play "Dname's game" with friends between videos. Price is in the range of one snack run.
Fans (cosmetics ₩1,000–5,000) Blocky avatars, reveal effects, emotes. No pay-to-win. Status inside the room, and on Dname's stream.
Teachers / academies (group licence ₩9,900/mo) Ad-free classroom mode, custom quiz packs on school topics Phones-as-buzzers party games are already used in classrooms. Jackbox and Kahoot prove the format.

Prizes are always sponsor-funded with free entry. Players never pay to enter, and nothing random can be bought. This keeps it out of gambling/lottery rules and is a hard design rule, not a later fix.

2. Funnel (how players arrive)

Public counts (2026-11-06): 270K subscribers, median ≈1.7M views over the last 50 shorts, but recent uploads are rare and weaker (13K–755K). The model therefore uses lower view numbers than the median.

  1. Dname posts a short that is a game round ("Don't say ㄹ", "Find the diamond") and ends with "join this week's final: QR / link in the pinned comment".
  2. Viewers play the same round on their phones in the weekly live final. The best moments become his next short.
  3. Between finals, the Daily Challenge (live now: /?daily=1) catches viewers whenever they watch a short. Its shareable 🟩🟨⬛ result brings in friends.
  4. Players bring friends into private rooms (Fan Pass), and teachers find it through students.

Room cap and overflow (honest limit). One live room holds up to 300 phones (server cap; load-tested at 300 simulated phones, p50 0.46 s). A short that brings thousands of players cannot fit one final, so the plan is:

  • Everyone can play the same rounds in the Daily Challenge, which has no player cap (one storage object per day, answers are small counters). Built and live.
  • The weekly live final room holds up to 300 players; up to 21 reserved seats go to Daily Challenge qualifiers (top 3 per day), so the seats reward the most engaged fans. Qualifier BUILT (s62): top 3 per day get a signed seat link; the final room (?final=1) admits only seat holders, one player per seat. Opening the remaining places of the final to other viewers is planned.
  • If one final is not enough, several rooms can run the same episode in parallel and merge their results into one board (each room is already its own Durable Object). Planned, not built yet.

Why ₩2M per sponsored episode is a low assumption: creator-marketing guides quote YouTube sponsorships at "$10 to $50 per 1,000 expected views for a dedicated integration" and price a Short at 0.25–0.5× of an integration (1of10, Aug 2026), i.e. roughly $2.5–25 per 1,000 Short views. At the Base 800K views that is ≈$2K–20K (₩2.8M–28M) per sponsored Short; we use ₩2M (≈$1.4K, ≈$1.8 per 1,000 views), below the bottom of that range. These are vendor-blog figures for mostly US channels, not a Korean rate card, so the input stays labelled (A). Korean market check: a Korean business magazine reports that one promotion on a 1M+ subscriber Korean channel commonly costs over ₩100M, and that for startups and SMEs a channel above 300K subscribers is already hard to afford (Bizhankook, 16 Mar 2026). That points the same way: ₩2M per episode is well under Korean market prices for a channel this size.

* Recent reach = Base rates, but 24K views per short (the median of his three latest uploads: 755K, 13K, 24K) and no sponsor deals. It is the honest floor if his posting stays as it is now: most of that revenue is classroom licences. The Base case assumes the game becomes his regular content again (2 game shorts a month at 800K, below his 1.7M 50-short median). That is the bet, and the reason the product is built to make a short cheap to produce (Make a Short button, recorded "Dname Final").

What the model shows:

  • Sponsorship is the biggest line in the base case (≈40% of revenue). The game turns his audience into a sellable ad format without asking kids to pay. That matters most for a kids' audience.
  • Revenue depends on shorts featuring the game. The game is built to be his content, with a recording overlay (he makes shorts, not streams), keyboard-driven host and replayable rounds, so making that short costs him less effort than his current Minecraft setups.
  • Infrastructure is not a meaningful cost (README "Unit cost": ≈$1–5 per million player-episodes). Margin is set by payment fees and support.

What this tells us, honestly:

  • The model's sponsor income does not scale with views. At 24K views the table still counts one ₩2M sponsor a month, which is unlikely. A brand pays for reach, so the realistic low-views case is the "Recent reach" row (no sponsors, ₩3.4M a year).
  • For Dname this is extra income, not a replacement for brand deals. In the Base case the Dname + Extriver side averages about ₩3.1M a month, well below one brand deal for a channel his size (Korean press: ₩100M+ per promotion for 1M+ channels). What the game gives him is a new format he can sell: "play my brand's round with Dname", where the sponsor pays for minutes of play instead of a 5-second mention. The more sponsored finals he books, the more both sides earn.
  • Classroom licences are the least proven line. They are only 5% of the Base case, but 75% of "Recent reach". We keep them because classroom mode works without new uploads, but we would test them with 10 academies before counting on them.

4. Competitors and alternatives

Alternative What it does well Why Block Party fits Dname's audience better
Custom Minecraft/Roblox maps (what he does now) Rich 3D worlds, his brand look Viewers need a PC/console, an account and a server slot, so only a handful can play. Block Party: any phone, a link, hundreds per room.
YouTube Live Playables (Sep 2025) Built into YouTube, zero setup Only the creator plays; viewers watch and chat. In Block Party every viewer plays.
Jackbox-style party packs Proven phone-as-controller format that people pay for Generic content, bought per pack, meant for living rooms. Block Party is free, runs his formats and has a stream overlay.
Kahoot / classroom quiz tools Huge classroom reach Quiz-only (right/wrong answers). Block Party's rounds are social (greed, telepathy, luck), so anyone can win. That is the appeal of his videos.
Chat polls / prediction widgets Easy for streamers One question, no game, no score. Block Party has episodes, a season leaderboard and a one-click "Make a Short" button that turns each episode into a 9:16 recap video.

The moat is not the technology. It is his formats + his audience + the weekly final + the season leaderboard, which a generic tool cannot copy. 5. Costs and risks

  • Costs: Cloudflare Workers Paid ($5/mo) once past the free tier; payment gateway fees (≈3–10%, assumed 10%); question-pack writing and moderation (a few hours a week); a Korean game rating before paid features launch (GRAC/self-rating; to check).
  • Minors: paid features go through a parent (web payment with parental consent, gift cards/vouchers). Free text only appears in the "Don't Say It" round: blocklist, 16-character limit and a host veto.
  • Children's privacy: Korea's PIPA requires a legal guardian's consent to collect personal information from children under 14 (COPPA has a similar rule for US children under 13). Free play is designed to collect no personal information: a nickname and a random id stored only on the phone, no accounts, no ads SDKs, no chat. Nicknames shown on stream are filtered (English/Korean). Anything that needs an account or a payment (Fan Pass) goes through a guardian. To be confirmed with Extriver's counsel before launch.
  • Creator dependency: rare uploads would mean fewer players, which is the conservative scenario. The friend-room and classroom modes work without new videos.
  • Copying: the format is easy to copy. The moat is Dname's audience, his weekly final and the season leaderboard, not the code.

6. Milestones

  1. Month 0–1: Dname plays one episode on stream, posts the clip, and we measure the real join rate (replaces the (A) inputs).
  2. Month 2: Fan Pass + cosmetics. First sponsor pitch, with the measured numbers.
  3. Month 3+: weekly sponsored final, classroom mode, Korean question packs written with the creator.
